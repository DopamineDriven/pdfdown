- Four of every five pages in half the corpus are boilerplate that contains

## 3. The approach being replaced, and what to keep from it

The consumer's current ingestion service is internal and is described here
only in the abstract; no vendor, class or path names from it belong in this
public repo. A read-only survey of its extraction layer on 2026-11-03 gives
the shape of the problem.

### 3.1 How it works today

1. **Text.** A PDF text library yields words; a line reconstructor clusters
   them by baseline (3 pt), sorts by x, joins with single spaces and **throws
   the x positions away**. Everything downstream sees flat text, one string
   per page, and most parsers join the pages with `\n`, losing page identity
   too.
2. **Image-only front door.** Pages below a character threshold go to a
   separate billed lane: rasterise → cloud document intelligence → an LLM call
   that must cite word ids for every value.
3. **Classify.** 175 ordered regex rules over page 1 (letterhead names, URLs,
   tax ids, report-generator footer filenames) choose one of ~148 format
   slugs; first match wins.
4. **Route.** A vendor→parser map (seeded, aliased, and learned from verified
   deliveries) or the classifier's verdict selects one of **142 bespoke
   parsers**.
5. **Parse.** Each parser is regexes over the flat text. Shared gap-fillers
   run afterwards: a PO-number extractor (label and `PO`+digits on one line, or
   "the cell below the label" approximated by _line offsets_), an annotation
   channel for stamped POs, an asset-context extractor (registration, model,
   serial, customer labels). A header-total extractor re-opens the PDF and
   runs 33 ordered "total" regexes over whitespace-collapsed pages.
6. **Check.** Item sum vs header anchor: ≤ 1% pass, ≤ 10% review, else fail.
   A rule-based verifier scores 24 rules (arithmetic 0.5, format 0.2, vendor
   profile 0.3, structural advisory). Credit-ness is decided centrally from
   markers in the first 25 non-blank lines of page 1 and a `-CR` suffix on the
   invoice number; **sign never decides credit-ness**, and deductions on
   ordinary invoices stay negative.
7. **Escalate.** No parser, no items, blank invoice numbers, or a failed
   variance sends the job to an LLM lane which, for unknown formats, writes a
   new parser ("forge") that then needs human review before it ships. A
   versioning manifest hashes every parser source so each verdict traces back
   to the code that produced it.

Size: 277 C# files, ~89k lines, of which the 142 parsers are ~64k lines with
about 2,050 compiled regexes. The largest single file is the classifier's
rule table (2.3k lines).

### 3.2 Why it cannot converge

- **Parsers are keyed on templates, not vendors.** One MRO network needs
  seven parsers (site, template generation, final vs progress billing); one
  shop needs three because it has used three billing systems; one OEM's UK
  and Canadian entities have two parsers of 517 and 510 lines that share
  _no_ logic yet do the same five jobs twice (total anchor, credit detection,
  tax-residual reconciliation, item-row regex, summary fallback), and only one
  of them auto-detects European number format. 26 parsers key on report
  generator footer filenames; 18 parse the same "Time & Materials / Flat
  Rate / Total" layout separately. 77 parsers carry their own money helper,
  94 their own date helper.
- **The corpus has 560 header clusters** (§2.3) and the service has 142
  parsers after a long time. The tail grows faster than the forge.
- **Column collapse is the recurring failure.** 87 parser files discuss it
  and 25 mention two-column layouts; the "value below the label" and "last
  token of the previous line" helpers exist only because x was discarded in
  step 1. The one place that keeps x (a columnar ship-to extractor used by
  four parsers) re-opens the PDF to get it back; each job opens the PDF three
  to four times.
- **No page identity, no multi-page mechanism.** 117 parsers join pages,
  42 read page 1 only; continuation headers, repeated footers and bundled
  second invoices are handled, when at all, per parser.
- **The contract is thin where pdfdown is rich.** The parse result has no
  header-level invoice number, date, vendor or currency (they are repeated on
  each line item); currency is never output and only used as an adjacency
  guard; due date is essentially never extracted; ship-to is emitted by five
  parsers.
- **The LLM sits in the critical path of every first-time document** and its
  output is code, which is the most expensive artefact to review.

The image-only lane is the exception that proves the rule: it rebuilds rows by
y-band on positioned words and extracts totals with generic qualified-label
sequences (`total amount due`, `invoice total`, `balance due`, `total
payable`, with `sub` disqualifying). That is the closest existing prototype
of what pdfdown does natively on every page, native or scanned.

## 4. Thesis and division of labour

pdfdown stays a **project-agnostic PDF (and spreadsheet) extraction library**
with Node bindings. Its invoice engine is a generic reader: it finds labelled
values, parties, tables and totals by geometry and lexicon, and returns typed
fields with confidence and provenance (`InvoiceResult`). Everything that knows
about the consumer's business lives in the consumer's own monorepo:

| pdfdown (Rust, MIT, no network)                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Consumer monorepo (TypeScript)                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Layout per page (native, OCR, rendered, spreadsheet)                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Intake: fetch, format detection (pdf / xlsx), job queue, page-range chunking of long documents                                                                                          |
| `InvoiceResult`: fields, items, aircraft refs, work order, document type, per-field confidence and spans                                                                                                                                             | Vendor master lookup (`vendorId`), cost-bucket routing, `categoryId`, `rentalType`, zero-line drop, 0–100 score, wire mapper                                                            |
| Page roles (invoice / continuation / terms / attachment), bundle segmentation, boilerplate suppression                                                                                                                                               | Known-vendor knowledge as **post-processing**: predicting the expected currency, terms or cost buckets for a vendor and comparing them with what pdfdown read; never fed into the parse |
| Currency, date order and decimal separator inferred **from the document content** in the same Rust pass ("are there indicators on the page?": vendor address country, tax vocabulary, IBAN prefix, label language; §6.8), reported with their source | Acting on `currencySource` and warnings: accept, override from vendor knowledge, or route to review                                                                                     |
| Lexicon tables (en first; aviation vocabulary included because the shape needs it, not because one consumer does)                                                                                                                                    | Review routing by confidence; LLM only as a last-resort _reader of the layout_, never as a parser author                                                                                |
| Eval harness that reads expected-value files from a local directory                                                                                                                                                                                  | The expected values themselves (export of the Azure pipeline's payloads), per-cluster dashboards                                                                                        |

Rule of thumb: if a rule would be wrong for an invoice that is not the
consumer's, it does not go in pdfdown.

**No parsers, anywhere.** The vendor-and-variant parsers are not being
ported, re-expressed or generated; they are being removed from the equation.
Better PDF handling (positioned words, page roles, a lexicon with geometry)
makes them unnecessary. The clusters named in this note are **measurement
strata**, not code paths: there is no `if vendor == …` in pdfdown, and no
vendor-specific _parsing_ in the consumer either. The consumer may know its
vendors (expected currency, terms, cost buckets) and use that knowledge
**after** extraction, on pdfdown's output; it never reaches the parse. Inside
pdfdown the only steering is content-derived: the engine reads its own
priors (currency, date order, decimal separator, language) off the page in
Rust (§6.8). `InvoiceOptions` keeps generic deployment defaults for those,
never a vendor or template key.

## 5. Engine findings from today's run (phase IV blockers and shapers)

### 5.1 The OCR cascade trusts one native character

`minTextLength` defaults to 1, so a scanned page with a stamped PO number
(8 native characters) is reported `Native` and never OCR'd; the same happens
to a page with a 12-character header over outline text. Found on both
"misclassified" documents in §2.1 and on the 27 stamped pages in §2.4.

Fix (engine, before phase IV): decide per page by **coverage**, not count.
A page is OCR-worthy when native words cover less than ~2% of the page area
while image placements cover more than ~50%, or when it has no words and
heavy path content (outline text). Output stays one `PageLayout` per page, but
OCR words are **merged with** the native words rather than replacing them (the
native stamp is real evidence); `TextWord.confidence` already distinguishes
the two populations, and `source` becomes `Ocr`/`Rendered` with a warning
naming the kept native words.

### 5.2 Outline text needs the render tier even when a logo is present

A page of filled paths plus one small image currently goes to tier 2, OCRs the
logo, gets nothing useful, and may stop there. The coverage rule above sends it
to tier 3. Without PDFium these 29 pages are unreadable; that is the one
place where the optional render feature is not optional for this corpus.

### 5.3 OpenMP and OSD (carried over)

Unchanged from the 11-02 note: set OpenMP to one thread per worker inside the
package, then add `detect_os` as an orientation pre-pass with the confidence
retry as fallback and the previous page's rotation as a prior. Both before
any OCR timing is quoted again.

### 5.4 Barcode fonts and anonymous fonts

- Words set in a font whose name matches `barcode|idautomation|code ?128|code ?39|3 ?of ?9|postnet` are kept in `words` (they are glyphs) but flagged so the invoice engine ignores them. Add `TextWord.symbolic?: boolean` or a `PageLayout.warnings` entry per such font; prefer the per-word flag.
- `CIDFont+F*` names carry no weight; bold/italic stay `undefined` rather than `false` for them, and the invoice engine's "largest/boldest line is the vendor" heuristic must not depend on bold.

### 5.5 Boilerplate and page roles are first-class

The five-page cluster makes page classification mandatory, and it has to work
on a **single document** (no cross-document memory inside pdfdown):

- Running header on every page ("Invoice #: …" alone on a line) is a strong
  continuation signal.
- Terms pages: high word density, numbered clauses, almost no column
  structure, no amount column, legal vocabulary (warranty, liability,
  indemnify, governing law, jurisdiction). Score, don't hard-code.
- Attachment pages: different page size, image-only, or a different producer
  signature (fonts) than page 1.
- Purchase-order pages inside bundles (the 11-02 exemplar): label-rich,
  name the buyer as bill-to; they are evidence for PO number and aircraft,
  not for totals.

`PageSummary.role` (`Invoice | Continuation | Terms | Attachment | PurchaseOrder | Unknown`)
is the output; the invoice engine reads header fields from `Invoice` pages,
items from `Invoice` + `Continuation`, and nothing from `Terms`.

### 5.6 Everything else observed

- 121 documents mix page sizes and 26 start landscape: the table engine's
  column bands are per page and must not assume page 1's width.
- 75 empty AcroForm dictionaries: `form` on `PdfMeta` is correct and
  irrelevant here; phase I stays as evidence only.
- Zero embedded XML: Track 1 is dormant on this corpus and stays as is.
- Zero warnings and zero failures from the layout engine on 9,775 pages is
  the green light to build on it.

## 6. Phase IV, revised with evidence

The module plan in `notes/2026-11-01.md` §7.3 stands (`lexicon`, `dates`,
`amounts`, `identifiers`, `anchors`, `parties`, `tables`, `totals`, `mod`).
Additions and reorderings that the corpus forces:

1. **`pages.rs` — page roles** (§5.5) runs first and gates everything else.
2. **`segments.rs` — bundle segmentation**: split a document into
   sub-documents at role boundaries and producer-signature changes; the
   primary invoice is the first `Invoice` segment; other segments are
   reported in `InvoiceResult.segments[]` with their own type (`SupplierInvoice`,
   `PurchaseOrder`, `Attachment`) so the consumer can decide (the buyer's
   own PO pages are the best source for the PO number and the aircraft).
3. **Lexicon**: English first, with the aviation vocabulary that
   `AircraftReference` and `workOrder` already imply (make/model, S/N, MSN,
   registration/tail, ESN, work order/shop order/job, tech log, squawk).
   Seed the label tables from §3.3 — as a reviewed list in the repo, nothing
   copied from the service.
4. **Three named clusters as the first milestones** (measurement strata for
   one generic engine, never separate code paths): A (iText, 54% of
   documents), B (Qt/wkhtmltopdf, 9%) and C (the engine OEM's two templates,
   2% of documents, the largest amounts). Each gets a synthetic public
   look-alike fixture built by `examples/gen_fixtures.rs` (same geometry,
   invented text) so the behaviour is tested in CI without the real files.
   C is the hardest of the three and exercises most of the engine at once:
   European and North American number formats in one vendor, credit notes
   with absolute amounts, deposit credits as negative rows, tax-residual
   reconciliation, bilingual labels, a bundled second invoice, and the
   111-page detail invoice (§7.5 of the design).
5. **Totals before tables**: the wire needs the header amount (gross, else sum
   of lines); reconciliation gives it confidence. Line items come second and
   are where most of the long-tail variance lives.
6. **Credit determination** as `documentType` plus a `creditSignals[]`
   explanation (label hits, negative totals, "credit memo" in the top band).
7. **Confidence calibration** on the real corpus against reference payloads
   (§9): fields ≥ 0.8 must be right ≥ 95% of the time, measured per cluster.
8. **Content-derived priors, resolved in Rust.** Currency, date order and
   decimal separator are inferred from the document itself, in the same pass
   over the layout, because that is where the evidence is and because doing
   it in Rust is cheaper than shipping evidence to the consumer and deciding
   there. No company information is involved: the engine does not know who
   the vendor is, only what the page says. Currency resolves in this order
   and the result names the tier that won (`currencySource`):
   1. `Page` — an ISO 4217 code or an unambiguous symbol (`€`, `£`, `CHF`,
      `C$`, `CA$`) next to the amounts or in a `Currency:` label.
   2. `Content` — priors read off the page when it shows only `$` or
      nothing: the **vendor address country** (postal-code shapes, province or
      state lists, country names → ISO 3166, then a built-in country →
      currency table: CA → CAD, GB → GBP, DE → EUR …); **tax vocabulary**
      (GST/HST/PST/TPS/TVQ → CA, VAT/MwSt/TVA → EU or UK, "Sales Tax" → US);
      **IBAN country prefix** and **phone country code** in the remittance or
      header block; **label language** (a bilingual FACTURE/INVOICE page is
      Canadian-French before anything else is read).
   3. `Option` — the caller's generic `currency` option from `InvoiceOptions`
      (design §7.1), a plain default for a deployment, not a per-vendor
      value.
   4. `Unknown`, with a warning.
      Each prior is scored, not trusted blindly: a Canadian address with `USD`
      printed next to the totals resolves to USD at tier 1 and the address
      contributes nothing. `dateOrder` and `decimalSeparator` resolve the same
      way: unambiguous evidence on the page (a day > 12, a `1.234,56` token)
      first, then the address country's convention, then the option, then the
      locale default. `AddressField` gains `country?: string` (ISO 3166-1
      alpha-2) so the inference is visible in the result.
9. **Options** (`InvoiceOptions`): `pages?: PageRange`, `AbortSignal` on
   async entry points, `includeCandidates`, and the existing generic
   `currency` / `dateOrder` / `decimalSeparator` / `languages` defaults. No
   `hints` keyed on vendors or templates.

### 6.1 The downstream wire shape is already generic

A real post-processed payload from the current pipeline (one invoice; values
private) was checked field by field against `InvoiceFields`. Almost all of
it is plain invoice vocabulary that pdfdown can own; the rest is business
classification that the consumer adds on top. Nothing in it argues for a
consumer-specific shape in Rust.

| Wire field                                                                                                       | pdfdown                                                                                                                                                  | Who                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| `vendorName`                                                                                                     | `vendorName` as read from the page; on the wire it is a **lookup** of `vendorId` in the consumer's vendor master (id → name, country), not an extraction | pdfdown reads, consumer resolves |
| `invoiceNumber`, `invoiceDate`                                                                                   | `invoiceId`, `invoiceDate`                                                                                                                               | pdfdown                          |
| `invoiceAmount` (header gross, else sum of lines)                                                                | `invoiceTotal` / `amountDue`, reconciled against `items`                                                                                                 | pdfdown                          |
| `poNumber`                                                                                                       | `purchaseOrder`                                                                                                                                          | pdfdown                          |
| `invoiceType` (Invoice / Credit)                                                                                 | `documentType`                                                                                                                                           | pdfdown                          |
| `serialNumber` (aircraft)                                                                                        | `aircraft[].serialNumber`                                                                                                                                | pdfdown                          |
| `shipTo { name, address, city, state, zipCode, country }`                                                        | `shippingAddress` (+ `country?`, §6.8); city/state/zip split is a thin mapper                                                                            | pdfdown + mapper                 |
| `confidenceScore` 0–100                                                                                          | `confidence` 0..1 × 100                                                                                                                                  | mapper                           |
| `items[].partNumber`, `partName`/`description`, `quantity`, `unitPrice`, `amount`                                | `productCode`, `description`, `quantity`, `unitPrice`, `amount`                                                                                          | pdfdown                          |
| `items[].vendorLineId`, `serialNumber`, `condition`, `laborHours`, `unitListPrice`                               | **add** `lineLabel`, `serialNumber`, `condition`, `hours`, `rate` to `InvoiceItem` (generic service-invoice vocabulary)                                  | pdfdown                          |
| `items[].materialCost` / `laborCost` / `miscCost`, `categoryId`, `rentalType`, `isFlatRate`, `unitDiscountPrice` | cost-bucket and category classification                                                                                                                  | consumer                         |
| `invoiceId` (correlation key), `vendorId`, `fileName`, `createdDate`, `stateMessages`                            | job metadata and vendor master                                                                                                                           | consumer                         |

So the mapper is thin: rename, scale confidence, split the address, and
attach job metadata. `InvoiceItem` gains five optional fields; nothing else
in the Rust shape moves.

**A warning about that payload as a reference.** Checked against the
document served under its id (a 16-page copier scan that pdfdown OCRs at
0.84 confidence on page 1), the payload does not match it: none of its
values, not the vendor, the invoice number, the PO, the amount or the line
item, occurs on any of the 16 pages. A corpus-wide search explains it. The
"vendor" named in the payload is in fact one of the consumer's **clients**
(an aircraft owner) that appears on four other documents; the payload's
invoice number has the exact shape the consumer's own in-house reimbursement
invoices use (case number + tail number + initials); and its line item is a
connectivity subscription that appears on other reimbursement forms in the
corpus. So the payload is a plausible extraction of an **internal/client
invoice**, and the file served under that id is a different document (a
freight and parts bundle from a logistics company). Whether the source
system attached the wrong file or the payload was filed under the wrong id
is for the consumer to find out; pdfdown read the file it was given
correctly. It settles the §9 point either way: the pipeline's output is a
**comparison system**, not ground truth, reference payloads must be checked
against file content before use, and the eval needs a human-verified sample
before either side is scored.

## 7. The spreadsheet track (`xlsx`)

**Taken 2026-11-05** (design `notes/2026-11-05/0338.md`, plan `0353.md`), pulled ahead of
phase IV as its own track. What landed differs from the sketch below in these ways: one
`PdfDown` class opens PDFs and workbooks (`openAsync`, `format`), with `detectFormat` returning
`{ format, detail }`; `spreadsheetCells` exposes the typed cells as a flat union on `type`;
`layout` on a workbook yields one logical page per selected sheet (print area or content extent,
no page-break pagination); number formats are rendered by a real parser of sections and tokens
with an en-US fallback only where the code leaves a choice; `invoice` on a workbook returns
`source: "None"` with a warning until the shared engine runs on spreadsheet pages, so there is no
`extractInvoiceFromXlsx`; XML is read with `roxmltree`, not `quick-xml`. The PDF-twin oracle
remains a later note.

Facts from the 17 workbooks (structure only, read with a zip/XML probe):

| Property               | Observed                                               |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Application            | Microsoft Excel, all files                             |
| Sheets                 | exactly one per file, same sheet name in all 17        |
| Used range             | `A1:O54` to `A1:O83`; one outlier `A1:S135`            |
| Rows / cells           | 51–135 rows, 672–1,460 cells                           |
| Merged cells           | 98–187 per sheet (the visual layout is made of merges) |
| Column widths          | 14 explicit `<col>` entries per sheet                  |
| Formulas               | 23–137 per sheet, cached values present                |
| Shared strings / media | 54–113 / none                                          |

Per Andrew: every one of these is the same "Maintenance Reimbursement Form"
with a predictable layout and a single hourly rate. That is one template today,
but spreadsheets are the **only non-PDF format** that invoice handling has to
accept, for vendor invoices and for the internal/client invoices that are
coming next and are less uniform than the vendors'. So a dedicated one-off
parser is the wrong shape; the right shape is:

- **Projection onto `PageLayout`.** Cells become `TextWord`s positioned by
  cumulative column widths and row heights (Excel units → points), merged
  ranges become one word box spanning the merge, number formats render the
  displayed text (`text`) while `value` keeps the typed cell value, formulas
  use cached values (never evaluated), hidden rows/columns are dropped, the
  print area or the used range becomes the page, and page breaks (manual or
  from the page setup) split into pages. `TextSource` gains `"Spreadsheet"`.
- **Then the same invoice engine runs unchanged.** The lexicon and geometry
  do the rest; a labelled "Rate" cell next to a number is the same problem
  as on a PDF.
- **Scope**: `.xlsx` (OOXML) only; `.xls` and `.csv` are not in the corpus.
  Pure Rust (`zip` + `quick-xml`), WASI-safe, in the base package. pdfluent's
  `xlsx-extract` is a conceptual reference for the Office-XML-to-blocks idea
  and nothing more (AGPL; never copied); ours keeps geometry, which theirs
  discards, because the invoice engine needs it.
- **API**: `layoutFromXlsx(buffer)`, `extractInvoiceFromXlsx(buffer, opts)`,
  plus `sheet`/`sheets` selection in options, and a generic
  `detectFormat(buffer) → "Pdf" | "Xlsx" | "Unknown"` (PDF header vs zip magic
  plus `[Content_Types].xml`) so a consumer can hand pdfdown any buffer and
  dispatch on the answer. Format capability is pdfdown's; what to do with an
  unknown format is the consumer's.
- **Fixture**: a synthetic public look-alike of the reimbursement form
  (`examples/gen_fixtures.rs` can write OOXML by hand, it is only XML in a
  zip), never the real files.
- **Oracle**: the corpus holds 64 PDFs that are printed copies of the same
  form (§8). Projecting a spreadsheet and laying out its PDF twin must yield
  the same lines in the same order with the same label→value pairs; that is
  the private acceptance test for the projector, and it needs no expected
  values written by hand.

## 8. Internal / client invoices

The brief is that they vary more than vendor invoices, and the corpus
already contains a first sample of them, found while chasing the payload in
§6.1: **22 in-house maintenance reimbursement invoices** issued by the
consumer to its clients (1 to 15 pages, printed through Foxit and Microsoft
drivers; header fields in label:value form, a labour/parts/misc table, the
client named where a vendor invoice names the customer) and **64 maintenance
reimbursement form PDFs**, which are the printed twins of the 17 spreadsheets
in §7 (same template; one carries the same connectivity-subscription line the
§6.1 payload describes). That is 86 documents, 4% of the corpus, already in
hand for step 5 of §11.

Nothing above is vendor-specific, which is the point: the same engine, the
same `InvoiceResult`, a second slot in the eval harness
(`$PDFDOWN_INVOICE_CORPUS` may be a list), and at most a second set of
generic deployment defaults in the consumer. The one design change worth
making now so it is not retrofitted:
`InvoiceResult.parties` as a list of role-tagged parties (`Vendor | Customer |
BillTo | ShipTo | RemitTo | Issuer`) alongside the flat `vendorName` /
`customerName` fields, because on an internal invoice "vendor" and "customer"
are both the same organisation's entities and the flat fields alone lose that.

## 9. Evaluation

- **Reference**: the Azure pipeline's post-processed payload for every id,
  exported into the private directory as `<id>.expected.json` (one exists).
  Comparison runs through the thin mapper (`InvoiceResult` → wire shape) so
  both systems are judged on the same keys: `invoiceNumber`, `invoiceDate`,
  `invoiceAmount`, `poNumber`, `serialNumber`, `invoiceType`, `shipTo`, items
  on (`partNumber` | `description`, `amount`).
- **Metric**: exact match per header field; per-document precision/recall on
  items; totals reconciliation rate; calibration of confidence; all **per
  header cluster** and for the scanned/mixed subset separately, with cluster A
  never aggregated into the headline without being shown alone.
- **Two weightings, always both.** Document-weighted (every invoice counts
  one) and **amount-weighted** (every invoice counts its reference
  `invoiceAmount`), because the money is concentrated where the documents are
  not (§2.3, cluster C). A field error on one engine-overhaul invoice should
  cost as much as the money it moves. The harness also accepts a
  consumer-supplied list of _critical ids_ and reports that slice on its own
  line; pdfdown never knows why they are critical.
- **Where**: `scripts/invoice-eval.ts` (design §9), reading
  `$PDFDOWN_INVOICE_CORPUS` or the gitignored directory, writing
  `eval.json` beside `profile.json`, printing a table; never committed.
- **Runtime budget**: native corpus ≈ 15 s; OCR subset ≈ 1–5 min after the
  OpenMP fix; the 111/106/104-page documents are the §7.5 stand-ins.
- **Baseline honesty**: the reference payloads are an automated system's
  output, and the one in hand is wrong for its document (§6.1). Export them
  all anyway, since agreement between two independent systems is still
  evidence, but score against a human-verified sample stratified by cluster
  (A, B, C, scanned, tail) before any accuracy number is quoted; where the
  two systems disagree, that disagreement is the review queue.

## 10. Risks and open questions

1. The dominant cluster can make a mediocre engine look great; reporting per
   cluster is non-negotiable. The mirror risk is value concentration: a 5%
   error rate on cluster C can move more money than a 50% error rate on the
   tail, so the amount-weighted number is reported next to the document-
   weighted one every time (§9).
2. Outline-text documents require PDFium at runtime for a slice of the
   corpus; the deployment has to ship it.
3. OCR on 1-bit copier scans at 0.5–0.9 confidence will cap scanned-field
   accuracy; §12 target of 85% for scans may need the OSD and binarisation
   work first.
4. Bundles: when the vendor invoice and the buyer's PO disagree (PO number, amount),
   which wins is a business rule, not pdfdown's call; the engine reports both
   with provenance.
5. The spreadsheet projector's fidelity depends on Excel's column-width
   semantics (character widths of the default font); approximate is fine for
   geometry-by-rank but column bands must be monotone and stable.
6. Client invoices are unseen; everything in §8 is a guess until a sample
   lands.
